Gross National Happiness (GNH) in Bhutan

Gross National Happiness (GNH) is one of the most important development philosophies of Bhutan. It was introduced by the Fourth King of Bhutan, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, in the 1970s. The main idea behind GNH is that the true development of a country should not be measured only by money or economic growth, but also by the happiness and well-being of its people. Bhutan believes that economic development should go hand in hand with social progress, environmental protection, cultural preservation, and good governance.GNH is based on four main pillars: good governance, sustainable socio-economic development, preservation and promotion of culture, and environmental conservation. These pillars help Bhutan maintain a balance between modern development and its traditional values. For example, Bhutan places great importance on protecting its forests and natural environment while also improving education, healthcare, and living standards.GNH also considers many aspects of people's lives, including psychological well-being, health, education, time use, community relationships, cultural participation, environmental conditions, and living standards. Through these areas, Bhutan tries to understand whether development is genuinely improving people's quality of life.The philosophy of GNH has become an important part of Bhutan's national identity. It reflects the country's approach of development with values, where economic progress is balanced with happiness, culture, nature, and the well-being of future generations. Today, GNH continues to influence government policies and development planning in Bhutan.
