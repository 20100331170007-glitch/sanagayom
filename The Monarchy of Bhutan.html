The Monarchy of Bhutan

The Monarchy of Bhutan is an important part of the country's history, culture, and national identity. Bhutan became a hereditary monarchy in 1907, when Ugyen Wangchuck was crowned as the first Druk Gyalpo, or King of Bhutan. Since then, the country has been ruled by the Wangchuck dynasty, which has had five Kings.The Kings of Bhutan have played an important role in maintaining peace, unity, independence, and cultural traditions. They have also guided the country through modernization and development. The third King, Jigme Dorji Wangchuck, introduced many important reforms, while the fourth King, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, promoted the philosophy of Gross National Happiness (GNH) and began the transition toward democracy.Today, Bhutan is a constitutional monarchy. The fifth and present King, Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck, became King in 2006. The Constitution of Bhutan was adopted in 2008, establishing a democratic system in which the King remains the head of state while elected representatives govern the country.The monarchy continues to have a special place in the hearts of many Bhutanese people. The Kings are respected for their leadership, service to the nation, and efforts to protect Bhutan's unique culture and environment. The monarchy's legacy is closely connected with Bhutan's journey from a traditional kingdom to a modern democratic constitutional monarchy.
